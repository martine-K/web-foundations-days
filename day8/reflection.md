# Reflection

**The most difficult concept.** The hardest concept for me was thinking in trade-offs and scale at the same time. In the early days, I wanted to pick "the best" tool, but caching, read replicas and queues each solve one problem and create another, such as stale data or extra complexity. Estimating load made it click. When I worked out that QuickNotes needs only about 46 reads and 12 writes per second, I saw that the design should focus on availability, not heavy sharding. I overcame the difficulty by doing the numbers first, drawing each diagram by hand, writing one sentence about the problem each component solves, and then re-explaining the design in my own words. The TicketHub double-booking problem made transactions and unique constraints feel real instead of theoretical.

**What I would improve in my capstone.** Based on the feedback I received, I would improve how I explain my design choices out loud and how I handle failure. My architecture document lists the components well, but I could say more clearly why each choice was made and what happens when something breaks: a cache outage, a worker crash, or a replica falling behind. I would add a short failure table, monitoring and alerts, and a clearer rule for when the app should read from the primary instead of the replica. I would also rehearse a shorter, clearer walkthrough of the request flow.

**What I will learn next.** I want to build a real backend with Node.js and Express connected to a PostgreSQL database, so the designs in my documents become working code. After that I will study authentication, automated testing, Docker and deploying an app to the cloud, so I can run and monitor what I design.
